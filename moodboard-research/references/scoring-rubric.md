# Scoring Rubric (6 axes × 0–2)

Every candidate is scored on these six axes. Total is 0–12. Keep only images with **total ≥ 8** AND **at least 4 axes scoring > 0**.

## subject — 0/1/2
Does the image actually show the subject from the brief?
- 0: completely off-topic (e.g. brief is apartments, image is a car)
- 1: subject is present but secondary (e.g. a person photographed in front of a wall)
- 2: subject is the primary content (a whole room, a recognisable apartment scene)

## mood — 0/1/2
How well does it match the atmosphere words?
- 0: cold / sterile / opposite mood
- 1: neutral, doesn't fight the mood
- 2: clearly hits the warmth / cosiness / atmosphere the brief calls for

## composition — 0/1/2
Is it framed intentionally? A real photographer or designer made deliberate choices?
- 0: snapshot, accidental, real-estate-listing flash
- 1: competent but generic
- 2: strong composition — leading lines, considered negative space, editorial framing

## color — 0/1/2
Palette alignment with the brief.
- 0: clashing palette (e.g. neon vs. earthy brief)
- 1: neutral / partially aligned
- 2: full palette match (e.g. all earthy tones for an earthy brief)

## style — 0/1/2
Medium and stylistic alignment.
- 0: wrong medium / aesthetic (e.g. anime when brief is photographic)
- 1: right medium, generic execution
- 2: clearly the style the brief points to

## originality — 0/1/2
Is this a stock-photo cliché or something with a point of view?
- 0: tired stock image, exact composition appears 1000 times on Pinterest
- 1: competent but unremarkable
- 2: a fresh angle, a specific real space, an artist's voice

## Threshold

- **Total ≥ 8/12**
- **At least 4 of 6 axes > 0**

Below threshold → drop, even if total is high. The "≥ 4 axes" rule prevents one strong axis from carrying a lopsided image (e.g. perfect color, zero subject).

## Relaxed threshold (Pinterest fallback)

Pinterest's `alt` text is often sparse or SEO-spammy, so keyword-based scoring undershoots. The Pinterest scraper falls back to ≥ 6/12 when it can't fill the target, and sets `"relaxed": true` on those entries so the contact sheet renders them with a dashed border. Treat those as candidates for a human pass.
