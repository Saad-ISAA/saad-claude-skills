# Launches and announcement series

A brand's first posts, or a big release, are a **series**, not one post. Announcing everything at
once spends the moment in a single feed slot and buries each feature in a list.

## The default arc (adapt the length)

1. **Hello** — who you are and the problem you exist for. No feature list.
2. **Why** — the pain, told from the reader's side; why the old way fails.
3. **One feature per post** — each with a real screen, a concrete situation, a benefit first.
4. **Difference** — what you do that the alternatives don't (only what you can prove).
5. **Trust** — data, security, deployment, customers, whatever this audience needs to believe.
6. **Invitation** — the first direct call to action, once the series has earned it.

End each post with what comes next, so the series reads as one story. Space it (e.g. twice a
week) and keep the cards consistent: same templates, same voice, one idea each.

## Channels

- **Owned** (website, product, mailing list) is where every post should eventually lead.
- **Rented** (LinkedIn page, other networks) is where attention is — use it, don't depend on it.
- **Borrowed** (founders' and employees' profiles, partners, communities, press) reaches people
  the page can't yet. A new company page reaches few people; the team's own profiles reshaping
  the same story usually reach more.

## Readiness

Before launching, check: is the product ready to be seen (the screens you'll show work)? Is the
destination ready (sign-up works, the site says the same thing)? Is someone ready to answer
comments in the first hour?

## When the request is "announce everything in one post"

Say it once, clearly: one post with everything gets one moment in the feed and no single idea
lands; a short series gives each feature its own moment. Offer the series as the recommendation,
with the first post drafted, and do it as one post if they still prefer.
